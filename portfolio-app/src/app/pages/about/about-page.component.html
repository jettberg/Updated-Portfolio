Professional Background

Ten years of fine dining taught me how to read people, communicate clearly, and perform under pressure.

Before moving into software engineering, I spent nearly a decade working in fine dining. I worked for seven years at Gianni’s Steakhouse in downtown Wayzata, serving guests in one of Minnesota’s most demanding hospitality environments, and later worked as a lead server at Macanda. Both located in Downtown Wayzata, with the highest-level clientele.

That experience taught me how to communicate with a wide range of people, from longtime business leaders and owners to younger entrepreneurs, executives, clients, and private event guests. I learned how to adjust my communication style based on the person in front of me: when to be relaxed and conversational, when to be concise and professional, and when to simply listen.

In private events and high-expectation service environments, details matter. You have to stay calm, anticipate needs, solve problems quickly, and make people feel taken care of without making the situation about yourself. Those skills directly translate into software: working with teammates, understanding client needs, asking better questions, and communicating clearly when solving technical problems.

Client communication Adapting tone, pace, and detail to the person or situation.
Pressure management Staying composed in fast-moving, high-expectation environments.
Attention to detail Noticing small needs before they become larger problems.
Team collaboration Working with others to deliver a smooth experience under pressure.
